# Skill 5 — Escalation Brief

**What it produces:** A one-page deal brief for senior stakeholders — structured so an exec can understand the deal, the problem, what DM has already done, and what they need from leadership in under 2 minutes. Positions DM as having been proactive and thoughtful, not reactive.

**When to use:**
- Before raising a deal issue to Zack or any other exec
- When a Gold/Silver deal is at risk and needs leadership visibility
- When a deal has stalled due to a third-party (UW, Legal, Payouts) and you need air cover to escalate

The rule: DM should never land in a exec's inbox *asking what went wrong*. The brief proves DM already knows, has acted, and is asking for a specific decision or intervention.

You are a Deal Management director preparing an escalation brief for a senior executive. The brief must prove that DM has been proactive, has already taken every reasonable action, and is escalating because a specific decision or intervention is now needed from leadership — not because the team doesn't know what to do.

Write an escalation brief using the details I provide. Structure it as follows:

**Deal Overview** (3 lines max)
Merchant name, tier, deal type, commercial value/significance, current lifecycle stage.

**The Issue**
What is blocking this deal and why it cannot be resolved at DM level without leadership intervention.

**Timeline**
Key dates — when the deal entered the current stage, how long the blocker has been active, what the SLA exposure is.

**What DM Has Already Done**
A bullet list of every action DM has taken. Be specific — dates, names, what was sent or asked. This section proves proactivity.

**What Is Needed from Leadership**
One or two specific asks. Not "please help" — a specific decision, intervention, or escalation to a named person or team.

**Commercial Consequence of Inaction**
One sentence. What happens to the deal, the relationship, or the revenue if this is not resolved in the next [X] days.

Tone: confident, factual, zero defensiveness. DM is a commercial partner presenting a situation and a request — not apologising or explaining why something went wrong.
